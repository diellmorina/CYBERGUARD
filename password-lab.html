    Password Security Best Practices

    ✅ DO:

    • Use long passwords (12+ characters is better than 8)
    • Mix uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
    • Use unique passwords for each account
    • Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass)
    • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA/MFA)
    • Change passwords if you suspect a breach
    • Use passphrases (random words strung together)

    ❌ DON'T:

    • Share your passwords with anyone (not even IT support)
    • Reuse the same password across multiple accounts
    • Use personal information (birthdays, names, pet names)
    • Use sequential numbers or keyboard patterns (123456, qwerty)
    • Write passwords on sticky notes or documents
    • Click "Remember Password" on public or shared devices
    • Use single words found in the dictionary
    • Send passwords via email or chat

    🔐 Why This Matters:

    Weak passwords are one of the most common reasons for account breaches. A strong password is your first line of defense against unauthorized access to your email, banking, social media, and work accounts.

    📊 Password Strength Stats:

    • 8-character password: Can be cracked in minutes with modern hardware
    • 12-character password: Significantly more resistant to attacks
    • 16+ character password: Very difficult to crack
    • Unique passwords: Prevent compromise of multiple accounts if one is breached
    ⚠️ Important Note: This strength checker is educational only. The strength score is an estimate based on common patterns, not a guarantee of security. Even a "strong" password can be compromised if:
    • The website is hacked and passwords are stolen
    • You use the same password on multiple sites
    • Your computer has malware or keyloggers
    • You fall victim to phishing
